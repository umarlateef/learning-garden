# Inspired by Marty Cagan — Reading Notes

## The Core Problem: Waterfall Discovery

Most teams use **Agile for delivery** but **Waterfall for discovery** — this is the fundamental disconnect.

### Typical (Broken) Flow
```
Leaders/Customer → Ideas → Roadmap → Requirements → Design → Development → Testing → Deployment
       ↑ Waterfall                   ↑ Agile only
```

**The problem:** Agile only covers the delivery phase (dev → test → deploy). Technical people are parachuted in at the **development phase**, missing their valuable early insight.

### Why This Is Broken
1. **Ideas come from leaders/customers** — not from empowered cross-functional teams
2. **Engineers brought in too late** — they solve implementation problems, not discovery problems
3. **Requirements-driven culture** — teams focus on building what's told, not solving what matters
4. **No shared ownership** — PMs own "what", engineers own "how", nobody owns "why"

## The Fix: Empowered Product Teams

Cagan's solution: **Empowered product teams** where PM, Designer, and Engineer(s) work together from day one.

- Teams are given the **problem to solve**, not a feature to build
- All three roles participate in **discovery** — not just delivery
- Technical people bring early insight, reducing risk and enabling better solutions
- The team owns the **outcome**, not just the output

### The Three Prerequisites for Any Product
Every product must be:
1. **Viable** — Must work for the business (profitable, sustainable)
2. **Desirable** — Must work for the customer (solve a real problem)
3. **Feasible** — Must work for the engineering team (buildable with available tech)

Discovery is about finding solutions that meet **all three** — not just delivering specs.

## The Four Big Mistakes

Every product team should avoid these four mistakes:

1. **Solving the wrong problem** — The problem doesn't matter or isn't real
2. **Solving a problem nobody has** — There's no demand for the solution
3. **Building the wrong thing** — The solution doesn't actually solve the problem
4. **Building the right thing wrong** — Quality/usability issues prevent success

> *Cagan argues: Every product team is making some version of these mistakes, and the cost compounds.*

## Key Roles in an Empowered Team

| Role | Responsibility |
|------|---------------|
| **Product Manager** | Owns the "what" and "why" — market/customer understanding, product strategy |
| **Product Designer** | Owns the "how it feels" — usability, UX, solving the right problem for users |
| **Engineer** | Owns the "how it works" — feasibility, implementation, technical innovation |

None of them decide alone. The team **collaborates** to find solutions that are Viable, Desirable, AND Feasible.

## Feature Teams vs. Empowered Teams

| Feature Teams | Empowered Teams |
|--------------|-----------------|
| Given WHAT to build | Given the PROBLEM to solve |
| PM decides, Engineers build | All three roles collaborate |
| Delivery-focused | Discovery AND Delivery |
| "Build features" mindset | "Solve problems" mindset |
| Low innovation | High innovation |

## Innovation Framework

Cagan proposes a **three-layer innovation framework**:

1. **Incremental** — Small improvements to existing products (daily)
2. **Transformational** — Significant new capabilities (monthly/quarterly)
3. **Breakthrough** — New product categories or markets (yearly)

> *Most organizations focus almost entirely on incremental. True innovation requires investment in all three layers.*

## Key Quotes

> *"The most dangerous product in the market is a product that's being built that nobody wants."*

> *"The job of product management is to discover the right thing to build. The job of engineering is to build the thing right."*

> *"If you're not making mistakes, you're not innovating."*
